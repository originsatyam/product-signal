# Product Signal

### Turning scattered customer feedback into clearer product decisions

A product design project exploring how AI can help product managers analyse feedback and decide what to prioritise.

## 1. The problem

Product managers receive customer feedback from different sources. Before they can make product decisions, they often need to spend time collecting, sorting, and understanding that feedback.

The problem I wanted to explore:

> PMs waste time sorting feedback before they can make product decisions.

The challenge is not simply having too much feedback. It is turning that feedback into something useful for deciding what to do next.

## 2. The objective

I wanted to explore how AI could help PMs spend less time on manual feedback analysis and more time making product decisions.

My design objective:

> Save PMs time by turning scattered feedback into organised, AI-generated insights that support faster prioritisation.

The AI is not the decision maker. The PM remains responsible for deciding what to prioritise, deprioritise, or investigate further.

## 3. The solution

Product Signal is an AI-powered feedback analysis workspace.

The basic idea is:

Import feedback → Generate insights → Review evidence → Make decisions

The product takes feedback that would otherwise require manual analysis and presents it in a more structured way.

### What the PM gets

* Organised feedback instead of manually sorting everything.

* AI-generated insights that help identify recurring problems.

* Source feedback so the PM can review where an insight came from.

* A place to turn insights into product opportunities and decide what to do next.

The value is not just “AI generates insights.”

The value is:

> The PM can move from messy feedback to a clearer understanding of the problems, then make a decision.

## 4. The core workflow

1

Import feedback

The PM uploads the feedback they want to analyse.

2

Generate insights

Product Signal organises the feedback and generates AI insights.

3

Review and decide

The PM reviews the insight and its source feedback, then decides what to prioritise, deprioritise, or investigate.

## 5. The screen I would lead with

### AI Insights

This is the most important screen because it demonstrates the main value of the product.

It shows how the product turns feedback into something a PM can understand and use.

The important relationship is:

Source feedback → AI insight → PM decision

The insight is the processed output. The feedback is the raw material.

When the PM opens an insight, they can see the source feedback behind it.

> I would lead with the AI Insights screen because it shows how raw feedback becomes evidence-backed insights to support prioritisation.

## 6. One important UX decision

### Why I used cards for insights

Each insight contains multiple pieces of information, such as:

* Headline

* Description

* Confidence

* Number of mentions

* Trend

* Evidence

I chose cards because these pieces of information belong together.

A table could also display the information, but for this particular presentation, I felt cards would make it easier to read each insight as a complete unit.

> I used cards because each insight contains multiple pieces of information, and grouping them together makes the insights easier to read and scan.

The important part is the reasoning—not the fact that cards are always better than tables.

## 7. How I think about AI accuracy

AI-generated insights are not automatically correct.

My approach is to make the AI assistive rather than authoritative.

Each insight is linked to its source feedback, so the PM can review the evidence before making a decision.

> The AI supports the insight, but the PM verifies the source feedback before deciding.

This is a design choice, not a claim that the AI is perfectly accurate.

## 8. What I would validate

This project is assumption-driven. I have not conducted real user testing yet.

The most important thing I would want to test is:

> Does Product Signal actually save PMs time compared with their current way of analysing feedback?

For example, if a PM currently spends several hours sorting and analysing feedback, I would compare that with the time and effort required using Product Signal.

The question is not just whether the AI generates insights.

The question is whether the product creates a meaningful improvement in the PM's workflow.

## 9. What I learned

### Outcome first, not feature first

One of the biggest things I learned while working on this project was to start with the outcome, not the feature.

Initially, I was thinking about things like:

* AI accuracy

* Organised data

* Source feedback

* Good UX

* Beautiful UI

Those are important, but they are not the main objective.

The main objective is simpler:

> I wanted to save PMs time.

Everything else should support that goal.

### Why this matters

A feature is not valuable just because it exists.

It is valuable when it helps the user achieve something important.

AI insights are the feature.

Saving PM time is the outcome.

That distinction changed how I think about product design.

## 10. My role and approach

I designed and built Product Signal as a personal product design project.

My focus was on:

* Understanding the problem.

* Defining the user and business objective.

* Designing the main workflow.

* Making the information easy to understand.

* Exploring how AI could support the PM's decision-making process.

* Building a working product concept.

I used AI to help build the product, but the design decisions and the product thinking were my responsibility.

## 11. Final takeaway

Product Signal is not about replacing the PM.

It is about helping the PM spend less time manually analysing feedback and more time making decisions.

> The goal is simple: turn scattered feedback into clearer product decisions, faster.
